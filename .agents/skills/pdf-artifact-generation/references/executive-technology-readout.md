# Executive technology readouts

Use for board, chairman and senior partner technology reviews. A monthly contributor
audit retains its separate evidence-led cadence; it is not the template for a strategic readout.

## Lead with the decision

1. Establish the audience, decision and evidence date before drafting. The first page must
   answer: what are we building, what has been demonstrated, and what should happen next?
2. Default to a short main readout with one takeaway per page. Use three or four pages as an
   editing budget, not a reason to omit material risks or shrink type. Honour the user's scope.
3. Lead with customer value and technology direction. Explain the relevance to the reader's
   industry without implying a partnership, endorsement or unmeasured commercial outcome.
4. Keep each point once. Remove repeated captions, generic praise, feature inventories and
   operator detail. Commit hashes, test totals, filenames and deployment receipts belong in
   supporting evidence unless the requested decision specifically depends on them.
5. Close with a clear recommendation and ordered milestones with observable exit outcomes.
   Do not invent delivery dates, adoption, reliability percentages or completion scores.
6. For a whole-system readout, establish the owner experience and the complete execution,
   information, authority and lifecycle flow before discussing individual cloud results.
   Show supported providers at the same abstraction level. An acceptance anecdote is
   supporting evidence; it must not become the platform narrative.

## Keep evidence honest and readable

- Distinguish demonstrated development behavior, production acceptance and future direction.
  State the material limit beside its claim; keep exhaustive test dispositions in the evidence file.
- Preserve source dates, units, sample sizes and measurement boundaries. A synthetic or isolated
  result cannot become a customer, production or hardware-performance claim.
- Label cost forecasts as estimates and state usage, included services and exclusions. Different
  model mixes cannot establish that one cloud is cheaper or that model quality is equivalent.
- Keep precise revisions and private receipts in the existing evidence sidecar. Use a short
  source note and shareable links in the readout; never include credentials or owner identifiers.

## Choose charts by the question

| Reader's question | Useful form | Required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How do measured options compare? | Direct-labelled horizontal bars or dots | Common units and scale; zero baseline for bars; comparable cohorts |
| How does usage change estimated cost? | Cost curves with labelled scenarios | Full pricing basis, fixed costs and exclusions; no unsupported break-even claim |
| What makes up one total? | Stacked bars or waterfall | Additive components of the same scenario; do not stack percentiles or overlapping charges |
| Where do authority and information reside? | Architecture or information-boundary diagram | Direction, ownership and current/future status; no invented flow volumes |
| How does a persistent agent use sleeping compute? | Lifecycle or swimlane diagram | Separate retained information, active compute, event wake and idle release; no implied device wake capability |
| What comes next? | Ordered milestone sequence | Exit outcomes, not fabricated dates or percentage progress |

Every chart earns its place by answering one question. A report does not need a chart quota
or a variety of chart types. Avoid decorative gauges, radar scores, 3-D charts, unequal scales,
and Sankey widths or area encodings without real quantities. Do not connect unrelated categories
as a time series. Use a reference target only when it is declared, and label it as a target.

## Render and review

Use the shared Foundation formatter, type and tokens. Quantitative figures may use standard
plotting tools and local vector assets; do not create a report-specific stylesheet or palette.
Direct-label values, use restrained colour for meaning, and keep captions short. Preserve
aligned gutters, readable labels and generous separation between headings, figures and sources.

Review twice: first as a reader with one minute to find the thesis, progress and recommendation;
then against the evidence for every number and limitation. Inspect every rendered page at normal
reading size for clipping, overlap, crowded charts, broken links and unnecessary repetition.
Keep the title and PDF filename aligned. Record the rendered proof in the existing artifact home.
